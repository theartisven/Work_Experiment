# User Personas — New Balance Korea (nbkorea.com)

**Based on the 20-Question Persona Interview Framework**
*Research context: existing customer + new segment exploration*

---

## Persona 1 — The Korean Lifestyle Loyalist

> *"I don't think of NB as a sports brand. It's just how I dress."*

**Name:** Jihye, 29
**Life stage:** Urban professional, 2–3 years into first real career job. Lives alone in a studio in Mapo-gu, Seoul.

---

### Core 10 Answers

**Q1 — How would you describe your work to someone outside your field?**
"I work at a mid-size company in marketing. Basically I make things look good and figure out what people want." She leads with output, not title. Identity is tied to taste and aesthetic judgment.

**Q2 — Who do you serve and who do you answer to?**
Reports to a team lead, produces for internal clients across business units. Decisions are her own within a defined budget. She buys mostly for herself, occasionally gifts to close friends.

**Q3 — Biggest goal in the next 3–6 months?**
Save enough to move to a slightly bigger apartment, build a more intentional wardrobe — quality over quantity. She's in "editing mode" on her life.

**Q4 — What would stop that goal?**
Impulse buys. Sales notifications that feel urgent. "I follow too many accounts. Sometimes I buy something and then feel bad about it." Her biggest threat is her own feed.

**Q5 — Last time she dealt with the problem domain?**
Needed new everyday shoes for commuting and occasional gym. Spent two weekends in stores and one week online before buying. Ended up with the 530 in a colorway she'd been tracking for months.

**Q6 — What does she work around?**
Size availability. Popular colorways sell out fast in her size (240). She screenshots and monitors manually because the notification system on the app doesn't always fire in time.

**Q7 — What has she tried that didn't work?**
Buying from resellers. Paid 30% premium once and regretted it. "I won't do that again. If it's gone, it's gone." But she still checks anyway.

**Q8 — Who does she trust when evaluating something new?**
Close friends first. Then a small set of Korean fashion/lifestyle accounts on Instagram she's followed for years. She is skeptical of brand campaigns but receptive to organic content from people she respects.

**Q9 — What kills a product in the first week?**
Slow loading on mobile, broken sizing guides, checkout friction. "If I can't check out in under two minutes on my phone, I leave." Also: product arrives and looks different from the photos. Trust breaks instantly.

**Q10 — What would she stop doing if she could?**
Manually tracking restock dates across multiple platforms. "There should be one place where I just set it and forget it and it tells me."

---

### Module A — Existing Customer Answers

**Q11 — What almost made her leave?**
A duplicate charge that took 10 days to resolve. "The product was fine. The CS experience was not. I almost switched to buying through a department store instead."

**Q12 — What does she use most vs. what she expected?**
Uses the wish list and mileage tracker far more than she expected. Came for specific products, stayed for the accumulated points and exclusive member offers.

**Q13 — What does she tell peers?**
"The Korea Exclusives are actually worth it. You can't get these colors anywhere else." She leads with exclusivity, not price.

**Q14 — What does she wish the product just did?**
Auto-notify when her saved size is back in stock. Also: a "complete the look" feature that shows apparel matched to the shoe she's looking at.

**Q15 — What is she doing outside the product?**
Uses KakaoTalk to share product links with friends for social validation before buying. Screenshot-saves items to a folder in her camera roll as a personal wishlist overflow. Neither of those behaviors is captured by the site.

---

### Persona Summary

**Primary motivation:** Belonging to a taste community. NB Korea is part of her identity, not just a store.
**Core pain:** Restock anxiety + mobile checkout friction.
**Trust signals:** Peer recommendation, organic social content, visual accuracy of product photos.
**Biggest opportunity for nbkorea.com:** Real-time size restock alerts + in-app social sharing → would reduce off-platform behavior and increase conversion speed.

## Persona 2 — The Korean Parent (Kids + Family Buyer)

> *"I buy for my daughter first. Then I end up buying something for myself."*

**Name:** Sungwoo, 38
**Life stage:** Married with two kids (ages 5 and 8). Mid-career manager at a mid-size logistics company. Lives in Bundang, commutes to Seoul.

---

### Core 10 Answers

**Q1 — How does he describe his work?**
"I make sure things get where they need to go, on time." Practical, systems-oriented. He thinks in checklists and efficiency. Shopping is a task to complete, not an experience to enjoy.

**Q2 — Who does he serve and answer to?**
Manages a team of 12. Reports to a director. At home, his wife handles most household decisions but he handles online purchases — she shows him what to buy, he executes the order.

**Q3 — Biggest goal right now?**
His daughter starts elementary school this year. He wants her to have the right shoes before the year starts. Secondary goal: find a good pair for his son whose feet grew two sizes in six months.

**Q4 — What would stop that goal?**
Wrong size. Kids' feet are hard to measure accurately at home. He's ordered the wrong size twice. The third time he drove to a physical store instead.

**Q5 — Last time he dealt with this?**
Ordered kids shoes online, size was off, initiated return, waited 5 days for pickup, reordered. Total process took 11 days. "It wasn't the worst, but I don't want to go through that again."

**Q6 — What does he work around?**
The size chart. It's inconsistent between adult and kids lines. He cross-references with offline store measurements and a note he keeps in his phone with each kid's measurements.

**Q7 — What has he tried that didn't work?**
A competitor's kids platform with a size recommendation tool. "It recommended a size too small. My daughter wore them once." He's skeptical of algorithmic recommendations now.

**Q8 — Who does he trust?**
His wife's judgment first. Then the ratings with the most reviews from parents. He reads low-star reviews before anything else — he wants to know what went wrong.

**Q9 — What kills a product in the first week?**
Wrong size + complicated returns. "If the return process is easy, I'll try again. If it's not, I won't come back."

**Q10 — What would he stop doing if he could?**
Driving to a store just to check if the online size is accurate. "That defeats the entire point."

---

### Module A — Existing Customer Answers

**Q11 — What almost made him leave?**
The return process after a sizing error. If it had taken longer or required more steps, he would have switched to Naver Shopping or a department store.

**Q12 — What does he use most vs. expected?**
Didn't expect to use the mileage system. Now checks it every time he logs in. Motivates him to consolidate family purchases into one account.

**Q13 — What does he tell peers?**
"The kids line is actually really good quality. Holds up even when they're hard on them." He recommends the durability, not the brand name.

**Q14 — What does he wish the product just did?**
A family size profile — store each person's measurements once and auto-suggest the right size per product. He would pay for this feature as a subscription.

**Q15 — What is he doing outside the site?**
Measures kids' feet with a tape measure, stores it in Samsung Notes. Compares to EU size charts on a separate website before buying. All of this happens before he even opens the NB app.

---

### Persona Summary

**Primary motivation:** Efficiency and accuracy. He wants to buy the right thing, once, fast.
**Core pain:** Size confidence on kids' products + return friction.
**Trust signals:** Honest reviews from other parents, easy returns, physical store parity.
**Biggest opportunity for nbkorea.com:** Family size profile / measurement vault feature. Would dramatically reduce sizing errors, returns, and "drive to store" fallback behavior.

## Persona 3 — The Foreign Visitor / Cross-Border Sneaker Buyer

> *"Korea has colorways that don't exist anywhere else. That's the whole point."*

**Name:** Yuki, 26
**Life stage:** Mid-level office worker in Osaka. Travels to Korea 2–3 times a year, largely organized around food, fashion, and K-culture. Plans purchases weeks before travel.

---

### Core 10 Answers

**Q1 — How does she describe her work?**
"Office admin. It's fine. My real life is the weekends." Her identity is built outside work — travel, fashion, content. She is a deliberate curator of things she owns.

**Q2 — Who does she serve and answer to?**
Operates independently in her personal life. Has a circle of 3–4 friends who travel together and share product finds. Group chat is the decision layer.

**Q3 — Biggest goal in the next 3–6 months?**
Plan her October Seoul trip, build a target list of Korea Exclusive NB colorways and limited items she can't get in Japan. She researches months ahead.

**Q4 — What would stop that goal?**
Items selling out before she arrives. She has missed specific products twice this way. She now monitors the NB Korea site from Japan before her trips.

**Q5 — Last time she dealt with this?**
Traveled to Myeongdong specifically for two NB items she'd tracked online. One was in stock. One had sold out the week before. She bought the available one plus two impulse picks she hadn't planned for.

**Q6 — What does she work around?**
She browses the Korean site without being able to read all the Korean copy. Uses translation tools, Google Lens on screenshots, and her friend who speaks Korean to decode product details and restock notices.

**Q7 — What has she tried that didn't work?**
Buying through a Korean proxy service once. Fees were high, took 3 weeks, and the product didn't match photos. Never again. She only trusts buying in person or directly from the official site now.

**Q8 — Who does she trust?**
Her own past purchases first. Then Japanese NB fan communities on X (Twitter) and LINE groups who post Korea trip hauls. She is deeply skeptical of influencer partnerships.

**Q9 — What kills a product in the first week?**
Discovering she could have gotten the same item without traveling. "If it's actually available in Japan, I feel cheated." Exclusivity is the entire reason she's there.

**Q10 — What would she stop doing if she could?**
The research phase. "I spend hours finding out what's actually Korea-only versus what they just market that way." She wants clarity on true exclusivity upfront.

---

### Module B — New Persona / Cross-Border Answers

**Q11 — What does she currently use to solve this?**
A manually maintained spreadsheet of target items, color codes, and estimated KRW-to-JPY prices. Supplemented by a Japanese fan community Discord channel with a dedicated Korea alert thread.

**Q12 — What would she trade to solve this better?**
A small monthly fee for an English/Japanese notification service tied to the NB Korea site — stock alerts, Korea Exclusive tags clearly marked, price in her currency. "I'd pay 500–1000 yen a month for that."

**Q13 — Who else has this problem?**
Her entire travel group. Also, based on the Myeongdong store data, almost every Japanese, Chinese, and Taiwanese tourist visiting Korea for NB has the same research gap.

**Q14 — What would make her trust a new product?**
If NB Korea officially flagged "Korea Exclusive" items with a dedicated tag or landing page in English and Japanese. "Right now I'm guessing. If the site told me clearly, I'd buy before I even arrived."

**Q15 — If it failed, what would she go back to?**
Her Discord group and the manual spreadsheet. "It works. It's just annoying. If an official solution wasn't clearly better, I'd stick with what works."

---

### Persona Summary

**Primary motivation:** Exclusive access to products unavailable in her home market.
**Core pain:** Language barrier + no reliable Korea Exclusive product filter + pre-trip inventory uncertainty.
**Trust signals:** Official brand designation of exclusivity, community validation from trusted peers, in-person store experience.
**Biggest opportunity for nbkorea.com:** A multilingual "Korea Exclusive" product hub with English/Japanese UI layer and pre-trip stock alert tool. Myeongdong store data shows this segment already drives 78% of that store's sales — capturing them digitally before they arrive is largely untapped.
