# Client Persona Questions — Agency / Consultant
**20 Questions · For forms or AI-assisted intake**
*Goal: understand the client's business, project context, constraints, and what success really means to them — before the brief*

---

## How to Use

Send all **10 core questions** before any project kickoff or proposal. Add **Module A** for project scoping and creative alignment. Add **Module B** for clients who've worked with agencies before and had a bad experience.

Questions are written for a self-serve intake form. If AI is processing responses, follow-up prompts tell it where to probe deeper.

---

## Core 10 — Always Send

**1. How would you describe your business to someone who's never heard of it?**

*What to listen for: Clarity of positioning. If they struggle to describe their own business simply, that's the first thing your work needs to solve — whether or not they've asked for it.*

*AI follow-up: "How do you want people to describe it after they've worked with you?"*

---

**2. Who makes decisions about this project — and who else has a say?**

*What to listen for: Whether your real client is in the room. If a CMO fills out the form but a CEO or board has final approval, you need to know that before you propose.*

*AI follow-up: "Has this kind of project been approved before, or does the budget still need sign-off?"*

---

**3. What's the one business problem this project needs to solve?**

*What to listen for: The difference between "we need a new website" (output) and "we lose leads because our site doesn't explain what we do fast enough" (problem). You want the problem.*

*AI follow-up: "How would you measure whether this project fixed that problem?"*

---

**4. What happens to the business if this project doesn't go well?**

*What to listen for: Stakes. Low stakes = flexible client with less urgency. High stakes ("we're presenting to investors in 90 days") = tight brief, fast feedback, defined success criteria.*

*AI follow-up: "Is there a hard deadline tied to this?"*

---

**5. What have you already tried? What worked and what didn't?**

*What to listen for: Past agencies, internal attempts, rejected concepts. You need to know what they've already rejected before you spend two weeks on a direction they've already ruled out.*

*AI follow-up: "Why did that approach stop? Was it the output, the process, or the relationship?"*

---

**6. What are you working around right now that this project should fix?**

*What to listen for: Manual processes, missing assets, disconnected tools, outdated materials. Workarounds often reveal the real brief hiding under the stated one.*

*AI follow-up: "How much time or money is that workaround costing you right now?"*

---

**7. What does a successful outcome look like — in your words, not in deliverables?**

*What to listen for: Outcomes ("our sales team feels confident using this") not outputs ("a 10-page deck"). Clients who answer in outputs need help translating to outcomes before work starts.*

*AI follow-up: "How will you know internally that this project was worth it?"*

---

**8. Who do you admire in your space — and what do you admire about them?**

*What to listen for: Aesthetic direction, strategic positioning, tone of voice, brand behavior. This is the fastest way to understand taste without a lengthy brand questionnaire.*

*AI follow-up: "Is there anything in your space — or outside it — that you'd want to feel like?"*

---

**9. What would make this project go wrong on your end?**

*What to listen for: Slow feedback cycles, too many approvers, shifting briefs, internal politics, budget cuts mid-project. Surface these now. Build them into your process.*

*AI follow-up: "Has that happened on a project before? What did it feel like from your side?"*

---

**10. What does your team need to feel comfortable handing this over to us?**

*What to listen for: References, portfolio examples, a specific discovery process, weekly check-ins, co-creation vs. fully outsourced. Tells you how to structure the engagement.*

*AI follow-up: "How involved do you want to be during the work itself — light-touch or closely in it?"*

---

## Module A — Project Scope & Creative Alignment
*Add for new projects where scope isn't defined yet*

---

**11. What's in scope — and just as importantly, what's out of scope?**

*What to listen for: Do they know? If they can't name what's out of scope, the project will grow. Use this answer to write the scope section of your proposal.*

---

**12. What's your timeline — and what's driving it?**

*What to listen for: Hard deadlines (product launch, conference, board meeting) vs. soft ones ("sometime this quarter"). Hard deadlines define your team allocation. Soft ones need a realistic push.*

---

**13. What's your budget range for this?**

*What to listen for: A real number, a range, or a deflection. Deflections ("we're flexible") usually mean they haven't decided yet. Ask a follow-up: "Has leadership approved a number, or is that still to be determined?"*

---

**14. How do you like to give feedback — and how quickly can you turn it around?**

*What to listen for: Detailed written notes vs. a call vs. "I'll know it when I see it." This determines whether your revision rounds will be fast and clean or long and vague.*

---

**15. Is there anything the team is afraid this project might look like or feel like?**

*What to listen for: "We don't want to look like a startup" or "please don't make it feel like a bank." Anti-preferences are as valuable as preferences. Clients are often clearer about what they don't want.*

---

## Module B — Clients Who've Had a Bad Agency Experience
*Add when the client mentions a previous agency relationship that went badly*

---

**11. What went wrong last time — and whose fault do you think it was?**

*What to listen for: Honest reflection vs. all blame on the agency. Clients who see their own role in what went wrong are easier to work with. Clients who blame entirely outward may repeat the pattern.*

---

**12. What did you wish the agency had done differently?**

*What to listen for: Communication frequency, proactivity, honesty about timelines, pushing back on bad ideas. Build those behaviors into your process from day one.*

---

**13. What would you need to see to trust a new agency within the first two weeks?**

*What to listen for: A quick win, clear communication, early draft showing direction, meeting deadlines. Whatever they say — do it in week one.*

---

**14. What made you decide to try again with a new agency rather than doing it in-house?**

*What to listen for: Capability gap, speed, objectivity, internal bandwidth. The answer tells you what you're being hired to be — execution partner, strategic lead, or outside validator.*

---

**15. What would make you stop working with us mid-project?**

*What to listen for: Missed deadlines, poor communication, feeling ignored, work that misses the brief. This is the contract-level conversation most agencies avoid. Have it early.*

---

## Scoring Guide for AI Processing

| Signal | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Answers Q3 with an output not a problem | Brief needs reframing before work starts |
| Hard deadline in Q4 or Q12 | Time-box scope; don't negotiate on deadline |
| Previous agency failure in Q5 or Q11 (Mod B) | Address process and communication first, work second |
| "I'll know it when I see it" in Q14 (Mod A) | Require examples or references before creative starts |
| Anti-preference answer in Q15 (Mod A) | Add to brief as a hard constraint, not a suggestion |
| Multiple approvers with no named final decision maker | Identify the real decision maker before proposal |
| Cannot name what's out of scope in Q11 (Mod A) | Scope creep risk — define it yourself in the proposal |
