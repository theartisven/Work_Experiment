# Client Persona Questions — Service Provider / Onboarding
**20 Questions · For forms or AI-assisted intake**
*Goal: understand what the client actually needs, how they work, and what would make them stay long-term — before service begins*

---

## How to Use

Send all **10 core questions** as part of your onboarding intake before the first session or delivery. Add **Module A** for clients with ongoing or recurring services. Add **Module B** for clients migrating from another provider.

Questions are written for a self-serve onboarding form. If AI is processing responses, follow-up prompts guide where to probe.

---

## Core 10 — Always Send

**1. How would you describe what you do — in one or two sentences, like you're explaining it to a friend?**

*What to listen for: How they frame their work tells you the language and level to match in every communication. A surgeon and a freelance illustrator both need your service, but they need completely different tones.*

*AI follow-up: "What part of your work takes up most of your time right now?"*

---

**2. Who else is affected by how this service goes — your team, your clients, your family?**

*What to listen for: Dependencies. If a whole team relies on their output, stakes are higher. If it's personal, communication can be more direct. This also surfaces who else might need to be looped in.*

*AI follow-up: "Is anyone else involved in decisions about how we work together?"*

---

**3. What's the main thing you're trying to get done or fix by working with us?**

*What to listen for: Primary outcome, not feature request. "I need more time back" is different from "I need this specific task off my plate every week." Both are valid — but they need different service designs.*

*AI follow-up: "Is there a specific result you'd point to in 90 days and say 'yes, that's working'?"*

---

**4. What would have to go wrong for this to feel like a mistake?**

*What to listen for: Fear of miscommunication, slow response time, quality issues, losing control over something they care about. These are your service SLAs before you've written them down.*

*AI follow-up: "Has that happened with a service provider before?"*

---

**5. What did you try before coming to us? Why did you stop?**

*What to listen for: DIY attempts, previous providers, tools they abandoned. You need to know what failed so you don't repeat it — and so you can position why your approach is different.*

*AI follow-up: "What specifically broke down — the quality, the communication, or something else?"*

---

**6. What are you still doing manually that you were hoping we'd handle?**

*What to listen for: Tasks they assumed were in scope but haven't confirmed. Surface these early — either add them to the service or set the right expectation now, not after they're frustrated.*

*AI follow-up: "How often does that task come up, and how long does it take you?"*

---

**7. What does good look like to you — not perfect, just good enough that you'd recommend us?**

*What to listen for: Their personal definition of value. Some clients want speed. Some want quality. Some want to never think about it. Knowing this shapes how you prioritize every delivery.*

*AI follow-up: "Is there a provider in another part of your life — not necessarily our category — who you think delivers exactly that? What do they do?"*

---

**8. Who do you usually ask when you're not sure if something is working?**

*What to listen for: Peers, mentors, online communities, internal team. This tells you who influences their opinion of your service — and who might be quietly advising them to leave if something goes wrong.*

*AI follow-up: "Do they have experience with services like ours?"*

---

**9. What would make you pause or cancel — even if the work itself was fine?**

*What to listen for: Billing confusion, poor communication, feeling like a low priority, slow responses. These are retention risks that have nothing to do with quality of work.*

*AI follow-up: "Has a service relationship ended for a reason like that before?"*

---

**10. How do you prefer to communicate — and how quickly do you expect a response?**

*What to listen for: Email vs. Slack vs. WhatsApp vs. calls. Response time expectations. Frequency of updates. If your defaults don't match theirs, set the standard now before it becomes a complaint.*

*AI follow-up: "Is there a time of day or day of week that works best for you for updates or check-ins?"*

---

## Module A — Ongoing / Recurring Service
*Add for retainers, subscriptions, or any ongoing relationship*

---

**11. What would a long-term relationship with us look like at its best?**

*What to listen for: How they imagine working with a trusted provider. High-trust clients want a partner who anticipates. Transactional clients want clean execution and clear invoices. Both are fine — just different delivery modes.*

---

**12. How do you like to give feedback when something isn't working?**

*What to listen for: Direct vs. indirect, written vs. verbal, immediate vs. collected. If they're the type to stay quiet and then churn, build check-ins into your rhythm that invite honest feedback.*

---

**13. What would make you want to expand the scope of what we do together?**

*What to listen for: Adjacent problems they haven't solved yet, capacity they want to hand off, growth they're planning. This is your upsell map — but only follow it once the core service is solid.*

---

**14. Is there anything in how you've worked with providers before that you'd want us to do differently from day one?**

*What to listen for: Proactivity, honesty about problems, structured reporting, less jargon. Free consulting on how to serve them better — take notes.*

---

**15. What would make you feel like you're getting more value than you're paying for?**

*What to listen for: An unexpected insight, a proactive heads-up, faster turnaround than expected, a resource they didn't ask for. Small things that signal "we're paying attention." Build a few of these into your standard delivery.*

---

## Module B — Migrating from Another Provider
*Add when the client is switching from someone else*

---

**11. What made you decide to switch?**

*What to listen for: The trigger. Was it a specific failure event, slow decline, or a better offer? A specific failure event means there's a clear bar to clear. A slow decline means the previous provider didn't notice — you need to build in check-ins.*

---

**12. What did the previous provider do well that you'd want us to maintain?**

*What to listen for: Anything they liked is something you must not break in the first 30 days. This is your minimum viable continuity list.*

---

**13. What did they do that you never want to experience again?**

*What to listen for: A direct list of things to avoid. These are harder to follow than a brief — they're behavioral, not tactical. Build them into how you staff and communicate this account.*

---

**14. How was the handoff from the last provider? Was anything lost or unresolved?**

*What to listen for: Missing assets, unresolved issues, bad feelings. If the handoff was messy, they may still be anxious. A structured, thorough onboarding from your side will reduce churn risk in the first 60 days.*

---

**15. What would it take for you to stop looking for alternatives once you've started with us?**

*What to listen for: The conditions under which they'd commit and stop shopping. Some clients need 30 days of smooth delivery. Some need a proactive moment that builds trust. Some need to see a specific result. Know which one this is.*

---

## Scoring Guide for AI Processing

| Signal | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Cannot define "good enough" in Q7 | Expectations are unformed — align before delivery starts |
| Mentions a specific past provider failure in Q5 or Q11 (Mod B) | Risk of repeat pattern — address the specific failure mode in your process |
| Lists a non-work communication tool in Q10 (e.g. WhatsApp) | Match their preferred channel or explain your policy clearly |
| "I'll just reach out if something's wrong" attitude | Passive churn risk — build proactive check-ins into your rhythm |
| Wants to expand scope (Q13, Mod A) before service has started | Expectation management needed — focus on core delivery first |
| Lists something the previous provider did well in Q12 (Mod B) | Protect that behavior; it's what they're paying you not to break |
| No clear answer on who else is affected in Q2 | Probe further — hidden stakeholders cause surprise escalations |
