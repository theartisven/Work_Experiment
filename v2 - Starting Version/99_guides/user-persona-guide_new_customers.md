# User Persona Interview Guide — New Product Research

**15 Questions · 40–60 Minutes**
*For exploring new audiences and validating unbuilt products*

---

## How to Use This

Run all **10 core questions** every time. Then add **Module B (5 questions)** for new persona or new product research.

Budget 2–5 minutes of follow-up per question. Every question is a thread-opener, not a checkbox. If an answer opens a new direction, follow it.

---

## Core 10 — Always Ask

> These questions work regardless of product, industry, or research stage.

---

**1. How would you describe what you do — to someone completely outside your field?**

*Why it matters:* How they frame their work reveals their identity and priorities more than a job title ever does. Listen for what they choose to lead with.

*Follow-up:* "Is that how you'd describe it to your manager, or is this more how you'd describe it to a friend?"

---

**2. Who do you serve in your role — and who do you answer to?**

*Why it matters:* Maps the human context around their decisions. Surfaces whether they're a solo decision-maker or caught between multiple stakeholders.

*Follow-up:* "When those two groups want different things, what do you usually do?"

---

**3. What's the one thing you most want to get done in the next 3–6 months?**

*Why it matters:* Goals stated in their own words are far more useful than category-level answers. Grounds everything that follows.

*Follow-up:* "How would you know you'd actually achieved it?"

---

**4. What would most likely stop that from happening?**

*Why it matters:* Fears and blockers drive behavior more reliably than aspirations. This reveals adoption risk, retention risk, and what they're protecting against.

*Follow-up:* "Has that happened to you before? What did you do?"

---

**5. Walk me through the last time you had to deal with [problem domain]. What actually happened?**

*Why it matters:* Behavioral past-tense questions surface real behavior, not idealized behavior. Works for analogous experience when exploring new personas.

*Follow-up:* "What did you try first? What did you do when that didn't work?"

---

**6. What do you work around in your current process rather than actually fix?**

*Why it matters:* Workarounds are latent pain that never gets reported. Maps unmet market needs for new personas.

*Follow-up:* "Why haven't you fixed it? Is it time, effort, budget — or something else?"

---

**7. What have you tried before that didn't work? Why did you give up on it?**

*Why it matters:* Failed attempts reveal what the market has already rejected and what your product needs to clear as a bar. Avoids re-learning lessons users have already learned.

*Follow-up:* "What would have had to be different for it to stick?"

---

**8. When you're evaluating something new — a tool, a service, a vendor — who or what do you trust most?**

*Why it matters:* Maps the influence network around their decisions. Identifies what channels and proof types actually move them.

*Follow-up:* "Can you give me an example of something you adopted recently because of a recommendation? What made you trust it?"

---

**9. What kills a product for you in the first week?**

*Why it matters:* The single most actionable onboarding question. Surfaces hard deal-breakers that stop adoption cold.

*Follow-up:* "Has that actually happened to you with something recently? What did you do after you gave up on it?"

---

**10. If you could stop doing one part of your current workflow and never think about it again — what would it be?**

*Why it matters:* Reveals the highest-frustration task they've accepted as permanent. Often the most emotionally honest answer.

*Follow-up:* "What's kept you from solving it already?"

---

## Module B — New Persona / New Product Research

*Add these 5 when exploring a new audience or validating an unbuilt product*

---

**11. What do you currently use to handle this problem — even if it's a spreadsheet, a workaround, or nothing at all?**

*Why it matters:* Every person has a current solution, even if that solution is "I just don't do it." Understanding what they already use tells you what you're really competing against.

*Follow-up:* "How did you end up using that? Was it a deliberate choice or did it just become the default?"

---

**12. What would you give up — time, money, effort — to solve this problem better than you do today?**

*Why it matters:* Willingness to trade reveals real priority. People overstate what they'd pay in the abstract; anchoring to time and effort gets more honest answers.

*Follow-up:* "Have you ever actually paid for something to solve this? What happened?"

---

**13. Who else in your world has this same problem?**

*Why it matters:* Maps the segment size and the referral network in one question. Also reveals whether this is a personal pain or a team/org-level problem — which changes the sales motion entirely.

*Follow-up:* "Do you talk about it with them? Have you ever tried to solve it together?"

---

**14. What would a new product have to do — or prove — for you to actually try it?**

*Why it matters:* Surfaces the trust and proof threshold before adoption. Reveals whether they need a free trial, a peer referral, a case study, or just a low enough price to take the risk.

*Follow-up:* "Is there anything that would make you try something even without that?"

---

**15. If a new product didn't work out — what would make you quietly go back to your old way instead of telling anyone?**

*Why it matters:* The inversion of the previous question. Reveals silent churn triggers and what "good enough" really means to them. People won't always tell you what failed — they'll just leave.

*Follow-up:* "Has that happened to you before with something you tried? What was it like?"

---

## Running the Interview

**Session length:** 40–60 minutes

**Note-taking:** Don't transcribe. Tag moments: `[pain]` `[workaround]` `[blocker]` `[trust signal]` `[quote worth keeping]`. Review tags after, not during.

**When to go off-script:** If a strong thread opens (like Q11 or Q14), stay there. A 10-minute conversation beats 5 surface-level answers.

**Recruiting:** Interview 3–4 new personas. Patterns appear within 4–6 sessions.

**Close with:** *"Is there anything you expected me to ask that I didn't?"* This surfaces blind spots and often produces the most honest feedback.
