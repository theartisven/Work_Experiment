# Client Persona Questions — Internal Stakeholder
**20 Questions · For forms or AI-assisted intake**
*Goal: understand what internal clients actually need, how they measure success, and what would make them champion your team's work — before the project starts*

---

## How to Use

Send all **10 core questions** before starting any cross-functional project, internal service, or team dependency. Add **Module A** when your team is delivering to an exec or leadership group. Add **Module B** when the stakeholder has had friction with your team or a previous internal team.

Questions are written for a self-serve intake form. If AI is processing responses, follow-up prompts guide where to probe deeper.

---

## Core 10 — Always Send

**1. How would you describe your team's main job to someone from a completely different department?**

*What to listen for: What they prioritize and what they consider background noise. A sales leader and a compliance officer may need the exact same output from your team — but weight it completely differently.*

*AI follow-up: "What does your team get measured on at the end of the year?"*

---

**2. Who else is counting on this project beyond your immediate team?**

*What to listen for: Hidden stakeholders, downstream dependencies, executive visibility. If the CFO is watching this project, you need to know before you set expectations.*

*AI follow-up: "Is anyone in leadership paying attention to how this goes?"*

---

**3. What's the business outcome your team needs from this — not the deliverable, the actual result?**

*What to listen for: The metric behind the request. "We need a new dashboard" is a deliverable. "Our team spends 4 hours every Monday pulling reports manually and it needs to stop" is the outcome. You want the outcome.*

*AI follow-up: "If this works exactly as hoped, what number or behavior changes?"*

---

**4. What happens to your team — or to you personally — if this doesn't come through?**

*What to listen for: Real stakes. A project with no consequences for failure will get deprioritized the moment something else comes up. Stakes tell you how seriously to treat the timeline.*

*AI follow-up: "Is there a specific date or decision point where this needs to be done?"*

---

**5. What has your team already tried to solve this? What got in the way?**

*What to listen for: Past internal attempts, previous team's involvement, budget that was spent. This tells you what not to repeat — and whether the problem is actually solvable with what you have.*

*AI follow-up: "Was there a reason that effort stopped? Who made that call?"*

---

**6. What are people on your team doing manually or inefficiently right now that this project should change?**

*What to listen for: The real daily cost of the current state. Manual workarounds and informal processes are evidence of where the pain actually lives — often different from where the request is aimed.*

*AI follow-up: "How many people are doing that workaround, and how often?"*

---

**7. What does success look like from your team's perspective — in plain language?**

*What to listen for: Outcomes in their language, not yours. An internal client who says "my team stops complaining about X" is giving you a real success metric. Use their words in your update reports.*

*AI follow-up: "How would you explain the win to your own manager?"*

---

**8. Who in your department do you look to when you're evaluating whether something is working?**

*What to listen for: The informal opinion leaders inside their team. If a senior analyst or a trusted team lead has concerns about your work, the stakeholder will hear about it — whether or not they tell you.*

*AI follow-up: "Would it be useful to loop them into feedback at any point?"*

---

**9. What would cause you to pull back support for this project, even if work was progressing?**

*What to listen for: Shifting priorities, budget cuts, leadership changes, scope that's grown uncomfortable. Internal projects die for political reasons more often than technical ones. Surface these now.*

*AI follow-up: "Has something like that happened on a past cross-functional project?"*

---

**10. How do you prefer to receive updates — and how often is too often vs. not enough?**

*What to listen for: Weekly digest vs. real-time Slack pings vs. monthly check-ins. Execs want concise summaries with risks flagged. Operational leads want detail and access to track progress themselves. Match their preference.*

*AI follow-up: "What's the most useful update format you've ever received from an internal team?"*

---

## Module A — Executive / Leadership Stakeholder
*Add when the internal client is a director, VP, or C-suite*

---

**11. What's the one thing you'd want to see in the first update that would tell you this is on track?**

*What to listen for: A leading indicator they trust. For some execs it's a milestone hit. For others it's hearing the right questions being asked. Build the first update around whatever they say here.*

---

**12. What's your biggest concern about how this project will go?**

*What to listen for: This is where political concerns come out. Team bandwidth, past disappointments, scope that's harder than it looks. Address the concern directly in your kickoff — don't wait for it to surface later.*

---

**13. How much do you want to be involved in the day-to-day versus getting a summary at the end?**

*What to listen for: Hands-on vs. hands-off. Either is fine — but a hands-off exec who gets surprised at the end becomes a hands-on exec at the worst moment. Calibrate accordingly.*

---

**14. What would make you actively champion this project internally to other leaders?**

*What to listen for: A win they can claim, a number that moves, a story they can tell. Build that win into the project plan. It's not cynical — it's how internal buy-in sustains over a multi-month project.*

---

**15. Is there anything politically sensitive about this project I should know going in?**

*What to listen for: Territorial disputes, budget competition, history between departments, a past version of this project that failed publicly. The answer here can change how you run the entire project.*

---

## Module B — Stakeholder With Prior Friction
*Add when there's been tension between this stakeholder and your team before*

---

**11. What's your honest read on how the last project between our teams went?**

*What to listen for: Whether they lead with facts or feelings. This question creates space for the real conversation. The answer tells you whether the relationship needs to be rebuilt before the work can succeed.*

---

**12. What did our team do that made it harder than it needed to be?**

*What to listen for: Communication gaps, missed expectations, slow delivery, over-promising. Take these seriously and repeat them back at the end: "So if we do X differently this time, that would help?" Then do it.*

---

**13. What would make you feel like this time is actually different?**

*What to listen for: A specific behavior, a specific milestone, a different point of contact. They'll tell you exactly what they need to rebuild trust. This is free consulting — don't miss it.*

---

**14. Is there anything your team needs from us that we haven't delivered in the past that made things harder?**

*What to listen for: Two-way accountability. Internal friction is rarely one-sided. If they surface something their team failed to deliver (approvals, content, access), acknowledge it. It opens the door to a more honest partnership.*

---

**15. What would make you genuinely want to work with our team again after this?**

*What to listen for: The conditions for a real partnership. If the answer is specific ("just hit the timeline, we've been burned on that twice"), it's achievable. If it's vague, ask a follow-up: "Is there one thing that matters more than anything else?"*

---

## Scoring Guide for AI Processing

| Signal | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Cannot define success in own language in Q7 | Alignment hasn't happened internally yet — don't start until it does |
| High personal stakes in Q4 | Stakeholder will be closely involved — increase update frequency |
| Past project failure in Q5 or Q11 (Mod B) | Diagnose what failed; don't repeat the same approach |
| Mentions executive visibility in Q2 | Treat every update as exec-facing; flag risks early, not late |
| Political sensitivity flagged in Q15 (Mod A) | Brief your team lead; this can't stay only with the PM |
| Hands-off preference in Q13 (Mod A) | Build a single clear escalation path; don't let things drift silently |
| Vague answer on Q10 (communication preference) | Default to weekly written summary + one async check-in; adjust after first cycle |
