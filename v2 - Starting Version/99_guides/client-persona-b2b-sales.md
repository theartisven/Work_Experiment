# Client Persona Questions — B2B Sales
**20 Questions · For forms or AI-assisted intake**
*Goal: understand who the buyer is, what they're trying to solve, and whether this is a real deal*

---

## How to Use

Send all **10 core questions** to every prospect. Add **Module A** when you need to qualify deal size and timeline. Add **Module B** when the prospect is comparing you to other vendors.

Each question is written for a self-serve form. If AI is processing responses, the follow-up prompts tell it what to dig into.

---

## Core 10 — Always Send

**1. How would you describe your role to someone outside your industry?**

*What to listen for: Do they lead with title or with what they're responsible for? People who lead with outcome ("I make sure our sales team has what they need") are more decision-oriented than people who lead with title.*

*AI follow-up: "What decisions do you own vs. influence?"*

---

**2. Who else is involved in a decision like this — and who ultimately approves it?**

*What to listen for: Number of stakeholders, whether procurement/legal is involved, and whether this person is the buyer or just the researcher.*

*AI follow-up: "What does the approval process usually look like for something at this price range?"*

---

**3. What's the business problem you're trying to solve right now?**

*What to listen for: Specificity. Vague answers ("improve efficiency") are early-stage. Specific answers ("our renewal rate dropped 8% this quarter and we think it's a handoff problem between CS and sales") are close to buying.*

*AI follow-up: "How long has this been a problem? What triggered looking for a solution now?"*

---

**4. What happens if this problem isn't solved in the next 6 months?**

*What to listen for: Whether there's a real consequence. No consequence = no urgency. A specific consequence ("we lose our renewal target and our CS lead is already on a PIP") = urgent deal.*

*AI follow-up: "Is there a specific event or deadline driving this?"*

---

**5. Walk me through how you've tried to solve this before.**

*What to listen for: Prior tools, failed implementations, internal attempts. This maps what they've already rejected and what bar you need to clear.*

*AI follow-up: "What made you stop using it / give up on that approach?"*

---

**6. What's your current workaround — even if it's messy?**

*What to listen for: Spreadsheets, manual processes, Frankenstein tool stacks. The messier the workaround, the higher the pain — and the more they're paying in hidden time cost.*

*AI follow-up: "How much time does your team spend on that workaround per week?"*

---

**7. What would a successful outcome look like 90 days after going live?**

*What to listen for: Whether they have a definition of success. No answer = no internal alignment yet. A specific metric answer = they're ready to buy.*

*AI follow-up: "Who in your company would be measuring that result?"*

---

**8. Who or what do you trust most when evaluating a vendor?**

*What to listen for: Peer referrals, case studies from similar companies, analyst reports, free trials. Tells you what proof to lead with.*

*AI follow-up: "Have you talked to anyone who's used [category of solution] before? What did they tell you?"*

---

**9. What would kill this deal on your end — even if you loved the product?**

*What to listen for: Budget freeze, competing priority, procurement requirements, integration blockers, legal review timeline. Surface these now, not at contract stage.*

*AI follow-up: "Is any of that in play right now?"*

---

**10. What does your team need to see before they'd fully commit to a new tool?**

*What to listen for: Pilot requirements, reference calls, security reviews, exec demo. Tells you what the sales motion actually needs to close.*

*AI follow-up: "Has your team done an evaluation like this recently? How did that go?"*

---

## Module A — Qualify Deal & Timeline
*Add when you need to assess deal size, budget, and speed*

---

**11. What's your timeline for having something in place?**

*What to listen for: Hard deadline vs. "whenever we find the right thing." Hard deadlines compress sales cycles. Open timelines need urgency creation.*

---

**12. Do you have budget allocated for this, or does that need to be approved?**

*What to listen for: Existing budget = faster. Needs approval = map the approval chain. No budget = either educate or deprioritize.*

---

**13. What size is the team or org that would use this?**

*What to listen for: This tells you deal size, complexity of rollout, and how much the contract is likely worth.*

---

**14. Are you evaluating anyone else right now?**

*What to listen for: Named competitors = real deal in motion. "Not yet" = you have time to shape the evaluation criteria. "Yes, and we're almost done" = you're late.*

---

**15. What's your single biggest concern about making the wrong choice here?**

*What to listen for: Fear of wasted budget, team resistance, integration failure, looking bad internally. The answer tells you what objection will kill the deal if you don't address it.*

---

## Module B — Competitive Evaluation
*Add when the prospect is comparing you against other vendors*

---

**11. What's the main thing you're trying to figure out by talking to multiple vendors?**

*What to listen for: Are they shopping on price, on features, on trust, on fit? The answer tells you what axis to win on.*

---

**12. What does your current front-runner do well that you'd want any solution to match?**

*What to listen for: This is your minimum bar. Anything you can't match here is a disqualifier.*

---

**13. What's missing from what you've seen so far?**

*What to listen for: This is your opening. The gap between what they've seen and what they need is where you win.*

---

**14. If price were equal across all options, what would make you choose one over the other?**

*What to listen for: Implementation support, company size/stability, integrations, UI, speed to value. The real decision criteria, not the stated ones.*

---

**15. What would "good enough to stop looking" actually look like?**

*What to listen for: The minimum viable product for this buyer. If you can hit it, say so clearly. If you can't, say so too — a fast no is better than a long dead deal.*

---

## Scoring Guide for AI Processing

When AI reviews responses, flag answers that match these patterns:

| Signal | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Specific consequence in Q4 | High urgency — prioritize |
| Named competitor in Q14 (Mod B) | Active evaluation — accelerate |
| "Spreadsheets" or manual process in Q6 | High pain, high conversion potential |
| No budget answer or "we'd need to find it" in Q12 | Slow or stalled deal |
| Multiple approvers in Q2 | Long sales cycle — map stakeholders early |
| No definition of success in Q7 | Not ready to buy yet — nurture |
| "We tried X and it didn't work" in Q5 | Listen for what failed — don't repeat it |
